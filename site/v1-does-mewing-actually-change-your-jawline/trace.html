# Does Mewing Actually Change Your Jawline? What the Research Shows

Short answer: nobody has shown that it does. Longer answer below, because "unproven" and "debunked" are different things, and the actual literature is more interesting than either camp admits.

## There's no trial

I searched for controlled trials of deliberate tongue posture reshaping the adult jaw. I used "mewing," "tongue posture orthotropics facial growth," and "tongue posture craniofacial growth randomized controlled trial." Nothing came back. The only mewing-specific PubMed hits were a couple of commentaries with no abstracts, plus an analysis of social-media discourse. That analysis describes the online claims as leaning on pseudoscientific language. [PMID:42674114]

So when someone posts a six-month progress pic as proof, that's an anecdote with lighting and angle variables. Treat the jaw-remodeling claim as unproven. That isn't the same as impossible, but nobody has put it to a proper test.

## Where the mewing idea comes from

The pitch isn't pulled from nowhere. Tongue posture does correlate with jaw shape in some people.

In one small cross-sectional study of about 40 teens and young adults in orthodontic care, people with Class III malocclusion had a lower tongue posture and a different arch shape. The authors wrote that tongue posture "is significantly lower in Class III subjects and is associated with the dentoalveolar characteristics of the maxilla and mandible." [PMID:22467568]

That's an association, and it's the whole basis for the "tongue shapes bone" story. It doesn't tell you which way the arrow points. A snapshot of 40 people can't show that changing tongue posture changes bone.

## The arrow might point the other way

A different study suggests it might. In growing children who got rapid maxillary expansion (an orthodontic appliance that widens the palate), the tongue ended up sitting higher afterward. The authors noted "a higher tongue posture can be obtained with RME in children with no reported respiratory disturbances." [PMID:19216585]

So change the structure and the tongue follows. That's the reverse of the mewing claim. The caveat is that these were kids with a hardware appliance, which tells you nothing about an adult practicing posture at home. But it's a good reminder that the "tongue is the cause" framing is an assumption, not a finding.

## What low tongue posture does seem to do

In healthy adults, habitually low tongue posture is linked to lower tongue pressure during swallowing. The authors suggest it may impair swallowing efficiency and pose a risk for age-related swallowing problems. [PMID:41992604] It's a functional finding, and it's cross-sectional, so no causation. It's also not about your face. Nothing in it says anything about jawline appearance.

## The adult bone problem

Most tongue-posture research is in children and adolescents, often in orthodontic treatment, and it doesn't transfer cleanly to adults doing this at home. Adult facial bones have largely finished growing, so large structural change from posture alone isn't supported. If you started at 16 and you're still going at 27, the odds that posture is the variable doing the work are low.

## Where tongue and mouth exercises do have evidence

Myofunctional therapy (exercises for the mouth and throat) has some trial support for a medical use in adults: less daytime sleepiness and possibly milder sleep apnoea. The Cochrane review says myofunctional therapy "probably reduces daytime sleepiness and may increase sleep quality in the short term." [PMID:33141943] It rates the certainty of the evidence as moderate to very low, so don't oversell it. It's also about airway function, not your jaw. If you snore, wake up wrecked, or suspect your breathing at night is off, this is a real reason to see a sleep physician. It isn't a reason to expect a sharper mandible.

## Don't bone smash

Bone smashing means hitting your own face to remodel it, and it's the part of this scene that actually worries people in the maxillofacial literature. A social-media analysis found #bonesmashing content was about 98% male, compared with 74% for #mewing. [PMID:42674114] Its qualitative review of the most-engaged posts identified risk minimization and normalization as one of four dominant narratives. [PMID:42674114] That study analyzed posts, not injuries, so it gives no injury rates.

You don't need a study to see the problem, though. Striking facial bones risks fractures, nerve damage, tooth damage and infection, and nobody has demonstrated any benefit. Don't do it.

## The forum effect

There's another cost worth naming. A qualitative study of roughly 8,000 forum comments found harsh self-critique and demoralisation in looksmaxxing communities. The authors argued the community subjects users to "masculine demoralisation, wherein they are seen as failed men and encouraged to self-harm." [PMID:40069550] This is interpretive and observational, so it shows a risk of harm, not how often it happens. But if scrolling those threads leaves you feeling worse about your face, that's data about the threads, not about your face. Body dysmorphic concerns are treatable, and a mental health professional is a legitimate move.

## What to actually do

- **If you want to try tongue posture, go in with accurate expectations.** It's cheap and I found no studies on harm from mewing itself. But don't force your jaw or tongue into a strained position for long stretches, since that can cause jaw or neck discomfort. Persistent jaw pain, clicking, headaches or trouble breathing means seeing a dentist or doctor.
- **If your bite or jaw shape bothers you, get it assessed.** An orthodontist or oral surgeon can look at real structural issues. Orthodontics and orthognathic surgery are the evidence-based routes for actual skeletal change.
- **If sleep or breathing is the issue, see a sleep physician.** Myofunctional therapy is one option they may discuss.
- **Skip the self-inflicted trauma.** No exceptions.

The honest summary: mewing isn't proven to work, isn't proven to be harmful, and has a plausible-sounding story built on correlations that may run backward. If you do it anyway, keep it low-stakes and don't make it the foundation of your self-image.
