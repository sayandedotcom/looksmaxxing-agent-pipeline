# Does Hard Gum Build a Jawline? The Best Adult Trial Says No

Jawline gum is one of the easiest looksmaxxing purchases to justify. It's cheap, it takes no skill, and the logic sounds right: muscles grow when you train them, so chew more and your face gets sharper.

The best adult data we have doesn't back that up. It does show something real, just not the thing you're buying it for.

## What the trial found

A 6-month randomised controlled trial in healthy adults had participants chew gum three times a day. At the end, there was no change in mandibular shape compared with baseline. [PMID:39215439] The researchers' own wording: "There was no statistically significant difference in MMT or mandibular shape compared to the baseline." [PMID:39215439]

MMT is masseter muscle thickness, and that didn't move either. Six months of gum chewing training did not significantly change it in these adults. [PMID:39215439]

If your theory was "the muscle gets bigger and the jaw looks wider," the trial tested the muscle, tested the jaw shape, and found nothing on either.

## What did change: bite force

Gum training did raise maximum bite force in the healthy adults. [PMID:39215439] At 3 months the training group's maximum occlusal force was significantly higher than at baseline and higher than the control group's (p < .001). [PMID:39215439]

That's a functional change, not a change in how the jaw looks. You can get better at biting down without your face looking any different. Anyone who tells you "it works, my bite force went up" is technically right and still has the same jaw.

## "But harder food makes chewing harder"

True, and there's a systematic review on it. In adults, harder food increased chewing parameters like muscle activity and the number of chewing cycles. [PMID:33142153] Two caveats. The included studies were of moderate to low quality, and the review didn't measure jaw appearance. [PMID:33142153]

So harder food makes your chewing system work more. Whether that adds up to a visible change in an adult face is a separate question, and that review didn't ask it.

## The rat problem

The "hard diet reshapes the jaw" argument usually traces back to animal work. Evidence that diet hardness affects jawbone architecture comes from young growing rats, not adult humans. [PMID:27608365] One such study found that mastication with a hard diet helped recover jawbone turnover and architecture that had deteriorated from poor chewing function during the growing period. [PMID:27608365]

That is a long way from "hard gum reshapes your adult jaw." It doesn't show that, and nobody should pretend it does.

## What about the specialty gum?

Mastic gum, Falim and the rest are marketed hard, and the marketing leans on exactly the claim above. We searched PubMed for "hard gum chewing mandible morphology jaw," "masticatory muscle exercise bite force masseter hypertrophy healthy adults," and "chewing gum masseter muscle thickness training." We found no study testing whether specialty jawline gum changes facial appearance in adults.

That's absence of evidence, not proof it fails. But the one adult trial we did find tested ordinary gum chewing and saw no change in jaw shape or muscle thickness, and the product pages aren't offering anything better.

## The downside question

The obvious worry with chewing a lot is the jaw joint. One small cross-sectional study of young adults found no statistically significant link between gum-chewing frequency, duration or chronicity and temporomandibular disorders (TMD). [PMID:40806875]

Don't read that as a clean bill of health. The study was underpowered and can't show causation, so it doesn't rule out jaw-joint risk from heavy chewing. [PMID:40806875] "We didn't detect a problem in a small sample" is about as far as it goes.

## What to actually do

- **Don't count on gum to sharpen your jawline.** The best adult trial found no change in jaw shape.
- **If you chew gum anyway, keep sessions moderate.** Stop if you get jaw pain, clicking, or headaches. If you like chewing gum, fine. Just don't treat it as a project.
- **See a dentist or TMD specialist** if you have jaw pain, locking, or clicking. Don't try to chew through it.
- **If the goal is a leaner face, look at body-fat management.** That's the more realistic lever than gum.
- **If you want structural change, book a consult** with a dentist or facial plastic surgeon. They can tell you what's possible for your face, which a forum thread can't.

## Bottom line

Gum will probably make your bite stronger. The evidence we have doesn't show it makes your jaw look different, and for specialty gum there's no adult appearance data at all. If you enjoy it, chew in moderation. If you're doing it for the jawline, put that money and effort toward something with better odds.
